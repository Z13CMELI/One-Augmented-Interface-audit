Three principles

Quiet AI. Explicit control. The human decision, front and center.

Three design principles, each with a research backing — and each validated by what planners told us during testing.

The most effective AI fades into the background.

Research on calm technology (Weiser & Brown, 1995; IDEO, 2024) consistently shows that systems earn trust by demanding the least attention possible. In One Interface, AI surfaces insights exactly when needed — without occupying the center of the screen or the planner's focus.

Weiser & Brown · PARC Xerox · 1995 — IDEO · 2024 — UX Magazine · Aug 2025

Control is the feature — not an afterthought.

When users can edit automation rules, set thresholds, and approve decisions before they execute, anxiety drops and adoption grows. The WHEN/IF/THEN rule editor, the override curve, and the high-confidence fast track in One Interface are not optional layers — they are what makes the system trustworthy to experts.

Nielsen Norman Group · 2025 — Stanford HAI · Humans in the Loop — USC / automation bias research

What planners see is the impact of their own decision.

Across all test sessions, the highest-satisfaction moment was the Validation Desk — where planners see their override's projected effect on service rate and margin before confirming. The interface deliberately foregrounds the human decision, not the AI action. That distinction is what makes planners feel capable rather than replaced.

One Interface user testing · Vague 1 & 2 · 4 planners — NNG: 63% trust AI that explains its reasoning
None of these principles constrain the product. They are what drives the 8.5/10 satisfaction score — and what makes the operational interface a tool planners want to use every day.
For leadership

Operational trust and strategic visibility are two different needs.

The operational interface is calibrated for the people who use it every day. That precision is what generates the outcomes leadership cares about. Decision-makers have a different question — and it calls for a different answer.

What the operational interface delivers

A workspace designed for speed, precision, and trust. Planners handle exceptions faster, make better-informed overrides, and spend less time on routine decisions. The CSAT evidence shows this approach works: 8.5/10 on V3, up from 6 on the first prototype.

What a leadership view would deliver

A dedicated surface showing what the system automates, what it flags, and what value it generates — in aggregate, in real time. A different audience, with a different cadence and different questions, served by a display designed for them.

The opportunity is not to make the planner interface more visible to leaders. It is to give leaders their own surface — one that answers their question directly: how much is automated, how much time is saved, and what does it protect.
Volume handled without human intervention — Zero Touch rate by zone and product family
Service rate protected by AI-detected alerts before they escalate to stock-outs
Average resolution time per alert — manual vs. AI-assisted comparison
Automation rule performance — success rate, override frequency, decision log

Proposed as a complementary surface — not a replacement for the operational interface
